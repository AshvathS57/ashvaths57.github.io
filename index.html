02 / JOURNEY

building things.

AGE 5

THE BEGINNING · ENGINEERING

Curiosity came first.

I started winding cables up and connecting them, trying to understand what happened when things were connected together. It was simple, but the instinct was already there.

EARLY YEARS

REPAIR · HARDWARE

The abandoned Acer.

I took an old Acer Aspire V5 that had been left unused by my family, replaced its battery and hard drive, and brought it back to life. It became one of the first machines I truly made my own.

EARLY COMPUTING

OPERATING SYSTEMS

Learning to modify systems.

My Hackintosh experiments began with manually built EFI folders and configurations. I later moved the work to a Dell OptiPlex and started learning how hardware, firmware and operating systems interact.

GAMING → SERVERS

MINECRAFT · CONTENT · NETWORKING

Gaming became infrastructure.

Minecraft started as something I played with friends and family. It led to my first online Minecraft server, a gaming YouTube channel, video editing and an early taste of running services for other people.

BUILDING

ELECTRONICS

From modifying to making.

A soldering iron opened another door. I built a mini MP3 player from a bare PCB, experimented with a solar-powered power bank, and started designing compact solutions for my own desk and home.

SERVER ERA

TRUENAS · PLEX · LINUX

The Acer became a server.

I converted the old Acer into a 24/7 TrueNAS server with 1 TB of storage, then added Plex for family media and began practicing Linux commands. The machine became my laboratory.

LOCKDOWN

SELF-LEARNING

Being stuck became an opportunity.

During lockdown in Chennai, I taught myself macOS, started a technology tutorial channel, experimented with electronics and audio, learned about UPS systems and transformers, and became more comfortable repairing hardware.

GRADE 6

GEMS FOUNDERS · SCHOOL

Gem of the Year.

I was awarded Gem of the Year for Discipline and Effort, and enrolled in the Duke of Edinburgh Junior Edition.

GRADE 8

FILM · CREATIVITY

French Francophonie — 1st place.

I filmed the GEMS French Francophonie film that went on to place first in the school competition. I also filmed and edited a class short film and its trailer.

GRADE 9

INNOVATION · ENGINEERING

A bias-aware pulse oximeter.

Using a Raspberry Pi and multiple sensors, I helped build a bias-aware pulse oximeter and cleared the first round of the GEMS Innovation Challenge from the whole grade.

POST-LOCKDOWN

NETWORKING · CCNA

From one server to an ecosystem.

Pi-hole, OpenVPN, CCNA study, Cisco Packet Tracer and a full Wi-Fi 6 mesh network turned my interest in servers into a deeper understanding of networking and infrastructure.

AUTOMATION

DOCKER · HOME ASSISTANT · ESP32

Making the home respond.

I learned Docker, deployed Home Assistant, built a JSON dashboard and repurposed an old tablet as a permanent control panel. A mechanical door lock later became a Wi-Fi system using an ESP32 and ESPHome.

AUDIO

DIY ENGINEERING

Learning sound by building it.

I went from experimenting with a living-room 5.1 system to building a DIY desk audio system around an Android car radio, amplifier, passive subwoofer and custom wiring — then kept iterating.

REPAIR → BUSINESS

HARDWARE · ENTREPRENEURSHIP

Broken monitors became an opportunity.

I started buying broken Dell office monitors, diagnosing and repairing them, and eventually buying discounted units and reselling them for profit.

ADS-B

RADIO · LINUX · INFRASTRUCTURE

Taking the server beyond my home.

I applied to host ADS-B receivers, received sponsored equipment, and built a dedicated Linux mini-PC in Chennai to support aircraft tracking and additional services.

TKS DUBAI

SCHOLARSHIP · GROWTH

An 83% scholarship.

After three rounds of interviews and coursework, I received an 83% scholarship for TKS Dubai, reducing the program cost from AED 27,000 to AED 3,000.

04 / STORY

It started with
a cable.

Long before servers, Docker containers or network diagrams, there was a five-year-old trying to understand what happened when cables were connected together.

That curiosity followed me into an abandoned Acer laptop. I didn't see a dead computer; I saw a machine I could bring back. Replacing parts led to operating systems, which led to Hackintosh experiments, PC building and eventually servers.

The pattern kept repeating. Minecraft became a server. A server became an excuse to learn Linux. Linux became networking. Networking became automation. Automation became physical systems with ESP32s and sensors. Audio became another engineering playground. Broken monitors became an opportunity to learn repair and business.

None of these projects were planned as a single curriculum. They grew out of questions I couldn't leave unanswered.

I don't think that instinct is going away anytime soon.
